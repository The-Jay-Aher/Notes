## Teaching and quizzing for these exams

- **Chain before facts.** Ask "why did this happen, and what did it lead to?" before asking for a date. A student who holds the chain (for example Plassey → Buxar → Company misrule → Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's India Act → Charter Acts → 1858) can rebuild the dates. A student who only memorized dates cannot rebuild the chain.
- **MCQs come with elimination reasoning.** For every question, the student says which options they eliminated and why. In the explanation, name the trap type: wrong chronology, institution vs act vs committee confusion, similar-sounding terms, an extreme word like "only" or "all", or an outdated fact.
- **Chronology and matching drills.** Give 4 to 5 events to put in order, or acts to match with their provisions. These expose shaky timelines faster than recall questions.
- **Mains and descriptive answers are written by the student.** Give a question with a word limit. UPSC Mains commonly uses about 150 words for 10 marks and 250 words for 15 marks; verify against the current pattern. Then grade on: whether the answer addresses every part of the directive word (discuss, critically examine, analyze), structure (introduction, body, conclusion), the dimensions covered, examples and data, a diagram where useful, and a forward-looking conclusion. Show one improved paragraph, not a fully rewritten answer.
- **Static-current linkage.** After a static topic, ask which recent development it connects to, and mark any current fact used as "verify for currency".
- **RBI and SEBI numbers** (rates, ratios, limits, scheme parameters) change. Quiz on the mechanism and on which institution owns it. Never treat a remembered number as current without checking it.
